# BY STAR - Project Demo presentation

The final [Project Demo PDF](https://by-star-team-challenge.pages.dev/presentation/by-star-project-demo.pdf) contains seven English slides, including the title and team introduction. It uses screenshots from the working application and identifies the fictional astronaut names, profiles and simulated mission data.

Use the PDF URL in **Link to Project Demo**. Use [the live application](https://by-star-team-challenge.pages.dev/) in **Link to Final Project**. Publishing these files does not submit the project to the competition.

## Slide content

### 1. BY STAR - Mission Health

Introduces health monitoring for crews when Earth support is limited. The cover names Team **by-star**, **Yoelvis Hernandez Brito** and **Blanca Nieves Sosa**, and the challenge **Create Health Monitoring Software for Astronauts on Space Missions**. The real project team is distinguished from the fictional astronaut profiles shown in the application.

### 2. Health in mission context

Shows the crew overview and alerts. Personal observations, the active breathing environment and crew input provide context for reviewing a reading. Each observation retains its source.

### 3. Crew and sensor visibility

Shows current and outdated wrist readings side by side. Six crew profiles keep assignments and personal references together; written quality labels, timestamps, units and device identity make the source inspectable.

### 4. Guided review and crew support

Shows Assist instructions, explicit outcomes and local crew handoff controls. A recorded request, acknowledgment and unresolved evidence remain separate states while the crew works through a concern.

### 5. Clinical reports with revision history

Shows a synthetic urine report held because its specimen identity does not match the order. A later accepted correction preserves the earlier report. Technical acceptance does not establish a diagnosis; the assigned simulated medical profile reviews the accepted input.

### 6. Exercise monitoring

Shows machine workload and cadence alongside independently sourced chest heart rate. Saved excerpts preserve timestamps, effort and data gaps. A dated inertial body-mass measurement remains separate from continuous exercise telemetry and body-composition assessment.

### 7. NASA evidence and crew guidance

Shows the App guide and explains the separate historical NASA reference: 16 ISS absorbed dose-rate records from [NASA OSDR RadLab](https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/radlab/gui/data-api/). Lunar telemetry and crew records are synthetic. Links identify NASA interface and exercise references, the public application and source repository. Future operational use requires authenticated sensors, protected medical records and validated mission procedures. The original code uses MIT, and OpenAI Codex development assistance is disclosed.
