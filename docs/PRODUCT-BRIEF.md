# BY STAR product brief

BY STAR helps a crew member understand a change in health telemetry, check it in context and record the next action. The concept addresses long-duration exploration where support from Earth may be intermittent and a crew member may have to act while tired, uncomfortable or working in a different environment.

The demonstration follows six crew members on the fictional SELENE-06 lunar construction mission. Two leave the descent cabin, work in suits, re-enter through the airlock and recover inside the habitat. Their breathing environment changes as they move. A simulated air monitor shows a change in cabin CO₂, a wrist monitor becomes stale and a crew member reports dizziness. BY STAR preserves each observation and its source. The crew can investigate the shared environmental incident and the individual concern without inferring a cause from their timing.

| Surface | What the demonstration shows |
| --- | --- |
| Health | A selected crew member and six-person overview, an interactive body map, breathing-zone readings, activity-aware trends, link state, shared alerts, a quick self-report, clinical results and the next scheduled check. |
| Assist | Steps with source and equipment context, explicit review outcomes, pause and resume, assigned work, a simulated crew handoff, clinical report review and a downloadable review record. |
| Profiles | Crew body maps, assigned instruments, illustrative preflight references, live replay of sensor packets, the planned schedule and project sources. |

The App guide is available from every page, with instructions for checking evidence, completing routines and asking for help. **Explore the post-EVA case** starts a separate practice replay with five stages and preserves the visitor's original session. The interface pairs written status labels with icons and color. A simplified presentation enlarges text and reduces visual density. Optional browser speech provides spoken readings and instructions, plus three explicitly activated read/navigation commands.

## What the demo measures

| Input | Variables in the replay | Operational precedent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crew wrist monitor | Heart rate (bpm), skin temperature (°C), and movement (counts/min) | ISS wearable studies monitor heart activity, temperature and motion using several types of devices. The body map locates the indicator or equipment while the source remains traceable to the wrist monitor. [NASA wearable research](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/iss-research/wearable-tech-for-space-station-research/) |
| Cabin, habitat, rover or suit monitor | Oxygen fraction (%), carbon dioxide partial pressure (kPa), and total pressure (kPa) | NASA monitors atmospheric constituents on the ISS. Suit requirements also identify oxygen partial pressure; BY STAR displays oxygen fraction in the simulated breathing zone. [ISS Major Constituent Analyzer](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20120010634) · [NASA spacesuit standard](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/11-0-spacesuits-vol-2/) |
| Mission and crew input | Crew identity, location, breathing zone, activity, link availability, assigned checks and reported concerns | NASA's Earth-Independent Medical Operations concept brings crew reports and onboard reference, wearable and environmental data together for the crew medical officer. [NASA EIMO concept](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250000009) |
| Quick check-in | Perceived rest, current fatigue, a concern category and approximate onset | NASA medical operations address work/rest scheduling, fatigue management and behavioral health. These demo questions are illustrative self-reports. [NASA medical operations](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-0-medical-operations/) |
| Episodic clinical report | A synthetic urine specific-gravity observation, order and specimen identities, instrument and method, quality, timestamps and revision history | NASA documents urinalysis around ISS EVA. The demo uses one illustrative analyte and a fictional mission instrument. [NASA astronaut medical evaluation, section 6.5.1](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/ochmo-std-100-1a-rev-a-signed.pdf#page=52) |

The replay shows the path from a reading to a crew decision. A late packet retains its sample time, a signal alert names the affected device, and a crew member can verify the evidence, request a handoff and record follow-up. The crew can still inspect the replay and follow the prepared guide during a simulated Earth relay interruption.

The proposal uses the same observation format for simulated packets and a future approved telemetry adapter. In operational use, continuous collection and the health record would live on a local mission service. Vessel, suit and habitat data would be provided by authorized read-only telemetry feeds. A linked device could propose a crew identity and require confirmation on a shared display. Low-bandwidth or unavailable Earth communications would remain distinct from the availability of local support.

The same incident and handoff workflow could support a future review involving several crew members, including possible infection or shared environmental exposure. Any operational health procedures, clinical decisions, exposure assessment and access to crew data require mission-specific authority and validation. The demo uses fictional procedures to show the flow of evidence and actions.

## From observation to recorded follow-up

Signal quality, the current monitoring condition and the human review outcome are distinct. A current wrist sample can still need review. A crew member records **Verified**, **Unable to verify** or **Further review needed** before completing a procedure. Completed steps remain visible as pending follow-up when their outcome, current condition or requested receipt still needs attention. **Start another review** preserves the previous history while opening a new review of the same item.

Monitoring retains the original evidence and permits another episode after a confirmed recovery and later recurrence. Sensor recovery requires usable new samples for the assigned channels. Environmental and resting-pulse recovery use two new comparable observations and a lower illustrative recovery boundary to avoid repeated alerts from small oscillations. These rules demonstrate event handling; their numeric boundaries are fictional and have no status as NASA clinical limits. The application never treats a completed review or a recovered signal as medical clearance.

The body view explains the individual's fictional preflight heart-rate reference and displays a difference only for a current comparable resting observation. Trend markers place changes of activity and review openings beside measured values. **Quick check-in** adds dated crew-reported context, and a reported concern creates its own Assist item. A post-EVA routine requires the crew member's check-in before that step can be recorded.

**Assigned to me** exposes work requiring the selected crew member's attention. The request and receipt remain distinct; receipt can be recorded only by the assigned profile while the simulated local link is available. **Review summary** brings the outcome, condition, evidence, context, self-reports, assignee and history together. The JSON download is a local export of fictional information.

## Use of NASA material

NASA-STD-3001 Volume 2 informs the visible stale or missing data labels (V2 10020), status text and symbols alongside color (V2 10045), and units displayed with values (V2 10046). The project uses these sections as prototype design references. NASA's EVA and environmental documentation informs the separation of activity, location and breathing environment. Mission links model the role of relays where direct communications are unavailable. [Crew interfaces](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/10-0-crew-interfaces-vol-2/) · [EVA risks](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/risk-of-injury-and-compromised-performance-from-extravehicular-activity-eva/) · [Lunar communications relays](https://www.nasa.gov/goddard/esc/lcrns/).

The [historical sample](../public/data/radlab-iss-sample.json) preserves 16 absorbed dose-rate records returned by RadLab for DosTel1 and DosTel2 on the ISS from the April 1, 2022 query window. The source timestamps and values are preserved in the file. It demonstrates traceable instrument data separately from the synthetic story. The replay has no real patient data. [NASA RadLab API documentation](https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/radlab/gui/data-api/).

## Clinical checks

The demonstration includes synthetic post-EVA urinalysis reports for Alex and Maya, planned by the fictional SELENE-06 mission. NASA documents urinalysis as part of the ISS medical evaluation around EVA. A NASA medical report also documents a portable blood analyzer in the ISS vascular contingency kit. NASA research operations support fecal sample collection for studies. Diagnostic equipment and consumables depend on the mission and vehicle; the demo's urine strip reader is an illustrative instrument. Blood and stool testing remain outside the implemented scenario. [NASA astronaut medical evaluation, section 6.5.1](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/ochmo-std-100-1a-rev-a-signed.pdf#page=52) · [NASA ISS contingency kit report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250001074) · [NASA research sample collection](https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/research-operations-integration/sample-collection-and-portable-systems-and-blood-collection-data/) · [NASA-STD-3001 Volume 1, V1 3004](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/3-0-health-and-medical-care/).

The planned check appears in **Next health task**, the report in **Health → My health → Clinical checks**, and its review in **Assist**. At the opening replay time, Alex's report is technically accepted and Maya's first report is held because its specimen ID does not match the order. At 06:42:46 UTC, a corrected Maya report arrives and retains its lineage to the held revision. Only the current accepted revision is available for medical review. Technical acceptance does not interpret the result or establish health status.

A separate versioned [JSON feed](../public/data/clinical-results.json) contains orders, instruments and episodic reports. The importer checks identities, units, timestamps, duplicate deliveries and correction lineage. Held observations are excluded from the accepted result display. Reports remain dated observations as the replay advances, and they use a different lifecycle from continuously refreshed sensor packets. See the [clinical data contract](CLINICAL-DATA.md) for supported fields and validation behavior. The design leaves room for a future mapping to HL7 FHIR diagnostic reports and linked observations; this prototype does not implement a FHIR service. [HL7 FHIR DiagnosticReport](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/diagnosticreport.html).

Noah is the assigned medical reviewer for the sample orders. Selecting his profile allows the review actions; selecting another profile leaves the report available for inspection. This is a public role simulation with fictional records, not an authentication or privacy boundary. Operational deployment would require authenticated identity, clinical authorization and protected records. The application does not autonomously prescribe a test or generate a treatment decision.

## Voice and crew communications

**Implemented interaction.** **Listen to current reading** reads the active crew member's pulse when available and **Listen** reads a procedure step. **Voice controls** offers explicit microphone activation with **Talk to BY STAR**, transcript feedback and **Run command** confirmation. The accepted commands are **Read my heart rate**, **Show my next check** and **Open Sensors**. Unsupported requests are rejected, and all actions retain screen controls. Voice commands query or navigate; outcome changes and support requests use visible controls. The Web Speech API is optional and the browser may use an internet speech service. Offline voice support is not assumed. [Web Speech API](https://webaudio.github.io/web-speech-api/) · [NASA human systems research on voice control](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210022146).

**Implemented handoff.** **Request support** and **Confirm receipt** record a simulated handoff in the browser, with recipient work visible in **Assigned to me**. Local-link interruption prevents receipt confirmation and leaves the request visible. A JSON export creates a local file. The prototype sends no external crew messages and plays spoken information only after user activation.

**Proposed delivery.** An onboard service would route messages to the assigned crew member, queue them during a link interruption, and distinguish delivery from acknowledgment. A shared alert can say that a review is pending. Clinical details belong in an authorized private view. The public replay uses fictional records. [NASA-STD-3001 Volume 1, medical operations](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-0-medical-operations/).
