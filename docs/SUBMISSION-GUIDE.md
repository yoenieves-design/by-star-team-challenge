## BY STAR preparation checklist

The following are practical recommendations for this project, separate from official eligibility requirements.

1. Open the actual team's portal page and confirm the registered team name, Team Owner, Local Event, challenge, and applicable deadline. A name alone does not establish the team's project URL.
2. Read the current official guides and inspect the portal's required fields before copying the submission text.
3. Use [PROJECT-SUBMISSION.md](PROJECT-SUBMISSION.md) as an editable content draft. Replace any placeholders with verified team details and fit the text to the actual portal limits.
4. Confirm that the public demo works without a login: [BY STAR live demo](https://by-star-team-challenge.pages.dev/).
5. Confirm that the source repository is public and readable: [BY STAR source code](https://github.com/yoenieves-design/by-star-team-challenge).
6. Review the published README, license, scientific sources, asset credits, and deployment instructions. MIT is the team's chosen code license; this checklist does not claim that NASA requires MIT. Third-party assets retain their own terms.
7. Prepare the required demonstration format only after verifying its official limits. Keep any supplemental presentation or longer recording clearly distinguished from the required demonstration.
8. Test every submitted link in a private browser window, including presentation or video permissions. Avoid links that require the team's account or expose private information.
9. Describe the prototype accurately: simulated telemetry and fictional crew are distinct from referenced NASA evidence. Identify implemented behavior, simulated communications, and future hardware integration separately. Do not claim clinical validation, NASA endorsement, or connected real spacecraft devices.
10. Identify NASA and other primary sources supporting the proposal, explain how they informed the design, and credit reused assets. Describe development assistance and AI use accurately; verify the official disclosure format separately.
11. Complete the portal's actual submission process before the applicable deadline. Confirm the resulting status in the portal; a published website or GitHub commit alone does not submit the competition project.
12. Save a record of the submitted text, artifact URLs, repository commit, and portal confirmation. Check the official rules before changing the submitted materials after the deadline.

## Suggested review of the demonstration

Use a short, understandable scenario to show what the astronaut can do and why it matters:

- Identify the crew member, mission context, current health information, and whether the underlying readings are available and trustworthy.
- Show a change or reported concern, the associated explanation, and the guided review workflow.
- Explain the difference between completing a workflow and confirming that the underlying condition no longer needs review.
- Show sensor verification, missing or delayed readings, and the distinction between clinical reports and continuous telemetry where useful.
- Explain the exercise example as a fictional replay informed by real spaceflight equipment, with body readings and machine readings kept distinct.
- End with the prototype's practical limits and the proposed path toward validated, authenticated onboard integration.

This demonstration outline is a project recommendation, not a verified judging rubric. The final format and content should be adapted to the official 2026 guide and the selected challenge.
